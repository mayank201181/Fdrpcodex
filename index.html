Guide

Most mistakes in these topics come from choosing the wrong whole. Every diagram below starts by naming the whole, then shows how to move forwards or backwards.

Reverse percentages

Discount: the sale price is not 100%

If a jacket is sold for 20% off at £80, the £80 is 80% of the original price, not 100%. Work backwards from 80% to 100%.

  1. Original price = 100%
  2. 20% discount means the sale price is 80%
  3. 80% = £80, so 1% = £1
  4. 100% = £100
Original price = 100%
80% paid = £80 20% off
Do not do £80 + 20% of £80 = £96. The 20% was taken from the original £100.

Profit and mark-up

Profit: the selling price can be more than 100%

If a shop sells an item for £120 after making 20% profit, the £120 is 120% of the cost price.

  1. Cost price = 100%
  2. 20% profit makes the selling price 120%
  3. 120% = £120, so 1% = £1
  4. 100% = £100
Cost price = 100%
100% cost = £100 20% profit
Selling price = 120% Cost price = 120 ÷ 1.2 = £100

The whole

Name 100% before calculating

Ask: “100% of what?” In discount questions, the original price is 100%. In profit questions, the cost price is 100%. In a test score, the total marks are 100%.

Fractions, decimals, percentages

Three languages, one value

A percentage is “out of 100”. A decimal is a part of 1. A fraction shows equal parts. Convert to whichever form makes the question easier.

3/4 0.75 75%

Ratio

Parts first, value second

For a ratio of 3:2 with 25 pupils, there are 5 equal parts. Each part is 25 ÷ 5 = 5 pupils.

3 parts = 15 pupils, 2 parts = 10 pupils.

Two-step percentage

Work on what is left

If you spend 40% of your money, you have 60% left. If you then spend half of what is left, that half is half of the 60%, not half of the original.

A reliable method

Use D-R-A-W for word problems

When a question feels like a story, slow it down and make the hidden structure visible.

DDecide the whole: what is 100% or the total number of parts?
RRepresent it with a bar, fraction strip or ratio table.
AApply each change in order: discount, profit, VAT, remaining amount.
WWork backwards when the final amount is given.

Common traps

Checks before final answer

Discount final price is smaller. If £80 is after 20% off, the original must be more than £80.
Profit final price is bigger. If £120 includes 20% profit, the cost must be less than £120.
Successive changes do not cancel. Down 10% then up 10% gives 99% of the original.
Ratios are not fractions until you add parts. In 3:2, the first share is 3 out of 5 parts.
