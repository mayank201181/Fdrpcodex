Step-by-Step Guide

FDRP means fractions, decimals, ratios and percentages. This guide starts with the easy language, then builds towards reverse percentages and multi-step word problems.

Chapter 1

What FDRP Means

Fractions, decimals, ratios and percentages are four ways to describe parts of a whole. Most harder questions mix two or three of these languages, so the first skill is knowing what each sentence is really saying.

The big idea: name the whole

Before calculating, ask: what is the total? What is 1 whole? What is 100%? What are all the ratio parts?

Words in questionThe whole is
18 out of 3030
20% discountoriginal price
20% profitcost price

Four languages for the same amount

The same value can be written in several forms. A strong student chooses the form that makes the problem easiest.

3/4 0.75 75% 3:1 split

In a 3:1 split, the first part is 3 out of 4 parts, so it is 3/4 = 0.75 = 75%.

Language clues

Quick worked example

18 out of 30 pupils walk to school. Write this as FDRP.

  1. Fraction: 18/30. Simplify by dividing by 6, so 18/30 = 3/5.
  2. Decimal: 3 ÷ 5 = 0.6.
  3. Percentage: 0.6 × 100 = 60%.
  4. Ratio walkers:not walkers = 18:12 = 3:2.

Chapter 2

Fractions

Fractions are about equal parts. They become difficult when the question hides the whole, asks for what remains, or mixes fractions with percentages and ratios.

Read a fraction correctly

The denominator tells how many equal parts make the whole. The numerator tells how many of those parts are being used.

3 shaded 5 equal parts 3/5

Core fraction skills

  • Simplify by dividing the numerator and denominator by the same number.
  • Compare fractions by using common denominators, decimals or benchmarks like 1/2.
  • Convert mixed numbers and improper fractions when adding or multiplying.
  • For a fraction of an amount, divide by the denominator and multiply by the numerator.

Forward problem

Find 3/5 of 60.

  1. One fifth of 60 is 60 ÷ 5 = 12.
  2. Three fifths is 3 × 12 = 36.
  3. So 3/5 of 60 = 36.

Reverse problem

3/5 of a number is 36. Find the number.

  1. 3/5 = 36.
  2. 1/5 = 36 ÷ 3 = 12.
  3. 5/5 = 12 × 5 = 60.
  4. The whole number is 60.

Fractions of what remains

This is a common two-step trap. If someone spends 1/3 of their money, the remaining amount is 2/3. If they then spend half of what remains, the half is half of the 2/3, not half of the original.

1/3 spent2/3 remains
spenthalf of remainingleft

Chapter 3

Decimals

Decimals are parts of 1 written with place value. They are useful for money, measures, calculators and percentage multipliers.

Place value

Ones.TenthsHundredthsThousandths 0.375

0.375 means 3 tenths, 7 hundredths and 5 thousandths.

Decimal habits

  • Line up decimal points when adding or subtracting.
  • Multiply by 10, 100 or 1000 by moving digits left in the place-value columns.
  • Round only at the end unless the question asks for an approximate answer.
  • Use zeros as placeholders: 0.4 = 0.40 = 0.400.

Fraction to decimal

Write 3/8 as a decimal.

  1. A fraction bar means divide: 3 ÷ 8.
  2. 3 ÷ 8 = 0.375.
  3. So 3/8 = 0.375.

Decimal of an amount

0.35 of 40 pupils walk to school.

  1. 0.35 of 40 means 0.35 × 40.
  2. 0.35 × 40 = 14.
  3. So 14 pupils walk to school.

Decimals connect to percentages

0.08 8/100 8%

To change a decimal to a percentage, multiply by 100. To change a percentage to a decimal, divide by 100.

Chapter 4

Ratios

A ratio compares parts. The most important step is adding the parts before sharing, because a ratio of 3:2 has 5 total parts.

Parts first

3:2 means 3 parts to 2 parts. Total parts = 5.

Ratio question types

  • Simplify a ratio by dividing all parts by the same number.
  • Share an amount by adding parts, finding one part, then multiplying.
  • If one part is given, use it to find the value of one ratio part.
  • Convert ratio to fraction by placing one part over total parts.

Sharing

Share £45 in the ratio 2:3.

  1. Total parts = 2 + 3 = 5.
  2. One part = £45 ÷ 5 = £9.
  3. First share = 2 × £9 = £18.
  4. Second share = 3 × £9 = £27.

Given one side

Flour:sugar = 5:2. Sugar is 140 g. Find flour.

  1. Sugar is 2 parts and equals 140 g.
  2. One part = 140 ÷ 2 = 70 g.
  3. Flour is 5 parts, so 5 × 70 = 350 g.

Ratio to fraction and percentage

If boys:girls = 3:2, then boys are 3 out of 5 parts. Boys are 3/5 = 0.6 = 60% of the group. Girls are 2/5 = 0.4 = 40%.

Chapter 5

Percentages

A percentage means parts per 100. Percentage questions become easier when she can move between mental methods, fractions and decimal multipliers.

Common percentage facts

PercentageFractionDecimal
50%1/20.5
25%1/40.25
75%3/40.75
10%1/100.1
12.5%1/80.125

Find a percentage of an amount

Use either a mental split or a decimal multiplier.

  1. 15% of £80 = 10% + 5%.
  2. 10% of £80 = £8.
  3. 5% of £80 = £4.
  4. 15% = £12.

Percentage increase

A score of 50 increases by 30%.

  1. 30% of 50 = 15.
  2. New score = 50 + 15 = 65.
  3. Multiplier method: 1.30 × 50 = 65.

Percentage decrease

Headphones cost £120 and are reduced by 10%.

  1. After 10% off, the customer pays 90%.
  2. 90% of £120 = 0.9 × 120 = £108.
  3. The sale price is £108.

Percentage change formula

To find a percentage increase or decrease from old to new:

change ÷ original × 100%

Example: from 40 to 50, the change is 10. Percentage increase = 10 ÷ 40 × 100 = 25%.

Chapter 6

Reverse Percentages

Reverse percentage questions give the final amount and ask for the original. The key is: the final amount is often not 100%.

Discount: the sale price is not 100%

If a jacket is sold for 20% off at £80, the £80 is 80% of the original price, not 100%.

Original price = 100%
80% paid = £80 20% off
80% = £80, so 1% = £1. 100% = £100.

Profit: the selling price can be more than 100%

If a shop sells an item for £120 after 20% profit, the £120 is 120% of the cost price.

Cost price = 100%
100% cost = £100 20% profit
Selling price = 120%. £120 ÷ 1.2 = £100.

Unitary method

A bike costs £255 after a 15% discount. Find the original.

  1. After 15% off, the sale price is 85%.
  2. 85% = £255.
  3. 1% = £255 ÷ 85 = £3.
  4. 100% = £300.

Multiplier method

After a 10% pay rise, salary is £1980. Find the old salary.

  1. After a 10% rise, the new salary is 110% of the old salary.
  2. 110% = 1.10 as a multiplier.
  3. Old salary = £1980 ÷ 1.10 = £1800.

Common mistake: adding 20% to the sale price

If £80 is after 20% off, doing £80 + 20% of £80 gives £96. That is wrong because the 20% discount came from the original price, not from the sale price. Always ask: what amount is the percentage taken from?

Chapter 7

Word Problems and Multi-Step Thinking

Harder questions are not harder because the arithmetic is impossible. They are harder because the story hides the order of steps. Use the D-R-A-W method every time.

D-R-A-W method

DDecide the whole: what is 100%, 1 whole, or all the ratio parts?
RRepresent it with a bar, fraction strip, ratio table or short equation.
AApply each change in the order it happens.
WWork backwards if the final amount is given.

Full walkthrough

Tara spends 40% of her money. She then saves half of what is left, which is £18. How much did she have at the start?

  1. Decide the whole: the starting money is 100%.
  2. After spending 40%, she has 60% left.
  3. She saves half of the remaining 60%, so she saves 30% of the original.
  4. 30% = £18.
  5. 10% = £6, so 100% = £60.
  6. Check: 40% of £60 is £24 spent, £36 remains, half of £36 is £18 saved.

Ratio then percentage

Girls:boys = 4:5. There are 27 pupils. One third of the girls bring lunch.

  1. Total parts = 4 + 5 = 9.
  2. One part = 27 ÷ 9 = 3 pupils.
  3. Girls = 4 × 3 = 12.
  4. One third of 12 = 4 girls.

Successive changes

A price falls by 10%, then rises by 10%. Is it back to the original?

  1. Start with £100 to test the idea.
  2. After 10% off, price is £90.
  3. 10% rise on £90 is £9, so new price is £99.
  4. It is not back to £100 because the second 10% used a different whole.

Final answer checks

Discount originalIf the price was reduced, the original should be higher than the sale price.
Profit costIf the selling price includes profit, the cost price should be lower than the selling price.
Ratio sharesAll shares should add back to the total.
Fraction of remainderMake sure the second fraction is taken from what remains, not from the original.
